PEAKS WORTH KNOWING

Famous Mountains

Mount Everest

8,849 m. The highest mountain on Earth, on the border of Nepal and China.

K2

8,611 m. A steep pyramid of rock and ice in the Karakoram, known as the Savage Mountain.

Matterhorn

4,478 m. The sharp pyramid on the Swiss and Italian border, one of the most recognized peaks in the Alps.

Mont Blanc

About 4,806 m. The highest mountain in the Alps and the site of the first major alpine ascent in 1786.

Denali

6,190 m. The highest peak in North America, in Alaska, famous for its cold and its huge rise from base to summit.

Kilimanjaro

5,895 m. Africa's highest mountain, a volcano that rises alone above the plains of Tanzania.

CAPTURE THE MOMENT

Mountain Photography Tips

Use Golden Hour

Early morning and late afternoon light gives mountains warm color and strong shadows.

Keep Batteries Warm

Cold drains batteries quickly. Keep your phone and spares in an inside pocket close to your body.

Add Scale

Including a climber or tent in the frame shows how huge the landscape is.

Handle Snow Glare

Snow can fool your camera into underexposing. Brighten the exposure slightly to keep snow white.

Try Night Skies

Far from city lights, mountains make a superb setting for star photos. Use a tripod and a long exposure.

Safety Before Photos

Never step near cliff edges or unstable snow for a picture, and stop only in safe places.

BE PREPARED

General Guidelines

Always check weather conditions before starting an expedition.

Carry appropriate clothing, food, water and emergency equipment.

Never climb alone on difficult or unfamiliar routes.

Inform someone about your climbing route and expected return time.

Respect local communities and protect the mountain environment.

Learn essential skills such as navigation, rope work and first aid from a qualified instructor before you go.

Allow time to adjust to altitude, and descend if symptoms of altitude sickness get worse.

Set a turnaround time before you start, and turn back when you reach it, even if the summit is close.

Check the avalanche forecast, and carry a beacon, probe and shovel when travelling in avalanche terrain.

Wear a helmet in places where rocks or ice may fall, or where a fall could injure your head.

Bring a way to call for help, such as a phone with offline maps, a radio or a satellite communicator, and know that coverage can be poor.

Check your gear before each trip, including ropes, harnesses, helmets and batteries.

DON'T FORGET

Day Climb Checklist

Backpack with a waterproof cover or liner.

Base layer, insulation layer and a waterproof shell, plus spare gloves and a warm hat.

Map, compass and a charged phone or GPS with offline maps.

Headlamp with spare batteries.

Water and high-energy snacks, with extra in case of delays.

Sunglasses or goggles, sunscreen and lip balm.

First aid kit and an emergency shelter or bivouac bag.

A whistle for signalling for help.

PROTECT THE MOUNTAINS

Leave No Trace

Plan ahead and prepare.

Travel and camp on durable surfaces.

Dispose of waste properly, and carry out everything you carry in.

Leave what you find, including rocks, plants and historic objects.

Minimize campfire impacts, and use a stove where fires are not allowed.

Respect wildlife, and observe it from a distance.

Be considerate of other visitors and local communities.
