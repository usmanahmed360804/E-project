DISCOVER THE ADVENTURE

What is Mountaineering?

Mountaineering, also known as mountain climbing, is the sport or activity of climbing mountains. It combines physical strength, technical skills, planning and knowledge of the mountain environment.

Modern mountaineering includes climbing rock, snow and ice-covered mountains. Climbers must understand weather, navigation, shelter, equipment and emergency procedures.

Proper planning and equipment reduce mountain risks.

Navigation

Maps, compass and GPS help climbers navigate safely.

KNOW THE DIFFERENCE

Hiking, Climbing and Mountaineering

Hiking

Walking on marked trails. It needs good shoes, water and basic navigation but no special equipment.

Climbing

Moving up steep rock or ice, usually with a rope, harness and protection. It focuses on a specific route or wall.

Mountaineering

The whole journey to a summit, which can include hiking, rock, snow and ice, camping and weather decisions.

THE VERTICAL WORLD

Altitude Zones

As you climb, the air pressure drops, so each breath holds less oxygen. At the summit of Mount Everest the pressure is about a third of that at sea level. The body needs time to adapt, which is why climbers ascend gradually.

High Altitude

1,500 to 3,500 metres. Most people feel normal, though some notice mild effects above 2,500 metres.

Very High Altitude

3,500 to 5,500 metres. Altitude sickness becomes common and gradual ascent is important.

Extreme Altitude

Above 5,500 metres. The body cannot fully adapt and stays for only limited time.

The "Death Zone"

Above about 8,000 metres. Climbers weaken quickly, and most use bottled oxygen.

WHERE PEOPLE CLIMB

Great Mountain Ranges

Roof of the World Ranges that draw climbers every year.

Himalaya

Home to Everest and most of the world's highest peaks, across Nepal, India, Bhutan and Tibet.

Karakoram

Includes K2 and some of the longest glaciers outside the polar regions, in Pakistan, China and India.

Hindu Kush

A rugged range through Afghanistan and Pakistan with many high, lightly climbed peaks.

The Alps

The birthplace of mountaineering, with Mont Blanc, the Matterhorn and the Eiger.

The Andes

The longest mountain range on land, with Aconcagua, the highest peak outside Asia.

Alaska & Rockies

Cold, remote ranges in North America, including Denali, the highest peak on the continent.

BEFORE YOU GO

How Climbers Prepare

Physical Fitness

Training combines endurance, leg strength and carrying a loaded pack over long distances.

Skills Training

Courses teach rope work, ice axe and crampon use, navigation and rescue techniques.

Route Research

Climbers study maps, guidebooks, recent conditions and the avalanche and weather forecasts.

Partners and Plans

Good teams share a plan, set turnaround times and tell someone where they are going.

Mental Strength

Patience, calm decisions and the discipline to turn back are as important as fitness.

Medical Check

A check-up and first aid training are wise before any high-altitude trip.

SPEAK LIKE A CLIMBER

Mountaineering Glossary

Belay

Managing the rope to protect a climber from a fall.

Crevasse

A deep crack in a glacier, often hidden under snow.

Col

A low point on a ridge between two peaks, often a pass.

Couloir

A steep, narrow gully on a mountainside.

Cornice

An overhanging mass of wind-blown snow at the edge of a ridge.

Bergschrund

The large crevasse where a glacier pulls away from the mountain wall.

Arête

A sharp, narrow ridge.

Serac

A tower or block of glacier ice that can collapse without warning.

Pitch

One rope-length section of a climb.

Moraine

Loose rock and debris carried and left behind by a glacier.

PROTECT THE MOUNTAINS

Responsible Mountaineering

Mountains are fragile places. Rubbish, human waste and damaged trails are growing problems at popular peaks. Climbers follow Leave No Trace principles to reduce their impact and respect the people who live in the mountains.

Leave No Trace

Plan ahead, stay on durable surfaces, dispose of waste properly, leave what you find, respect wildlife and be considerate of others.

Respect Local Communities

Support local guides and porters, pay fair wages and follow local customs and rules.

Help Others

A strong mountain culture means stopping to help a climber in trouble, even if it ends your own summit attempt.

QUICK ANSWERS

Frequently Asked Questions

Is mountaineering dangerous?

Yes, it carries real risk, including avalanches, falls and altitude illness. Training, good judgment and conservative decisions greatly reduce the danger but cannot remove it.

Can anyone become a mountaineer?

Most healthy people can learn the basics. It takes time, training and gradual experience rather than natural talent.

How long does it take to learn?

A basic course takes a few days, but gaining the judgment to travel independently in the mountains usually takes several seasons.

Do I need to buy all the gear first?

No. Many guiding companies and shops rent boots, crampons, helmets and ice axes, so you can try the sport before buying.
