PIONEERS

Historic Firsts

1786

First Ascent of Mont Blanc

Jacques Balmat and Michel-Gabriel Paccard reached the highest point in the Alps.

1953

First Ascent of Everest

Edmund Hillary of New Zealand and Tenzing Norgay, a Sherpa, reached the summit on May 29.

1975

First Woman on Everest

Junko Tabei of Japan reached the top on May 16.

1978

Everest Without Oxygen

Reinhold Messner and Peter Habeler showed that the summit could be reached without bottled oxygen.

1980

First Winter Ascent of Everest

Krzysztof Wielicki and Leszek Cichy of Poland reached the summit in February. In August, Messner made the first solo ascent, also without oxygen.

1986

All Fourteen Eight-Thousanders

Reinhold Messner became the first person to climb all fourteen. Jerzy Kukuczka of Poland followed in 1987.

2011

First Woman Without Oxygen

Gerlinde Kaltenbrunner of Austria became the first woman to climb all fourteen without supplementary oxygen.

2016

Winter Nanga Parbat

Simone Moro, Alex Txikon and Muhammad Ali Sadpara made the first winter ascent of the mountain.

2019

Fastest All Fourteen

Nirmal Purja of Nepal climbed all fourteen in six months and six days.

2021

First Winter Ascent of K2

A team of ten Nepali climbers reached the summit in January, the last 8,000-metre peak to be climbed in winter.

BEYOND THE NUMBERS

The Cost of Records

Records inspire people, but they are not the point of mountaineering. Behind most great ascents are teams of porters, guides and support climbers who often take the greatest risks and receive the least recognition. Sherpa and other high-altitude workers have made nearly every major expedition possible.

Many experienced climbers say the best result is returning home safely. A record is never worth ignoring bad weather, sickness or a teammate in trouble.

QUICK ANSWERS

Record Questions

Why do mountain heights differ between sources?

Measuring methods change over time, and snow depth on the summit varies. Nepal and China jointly announced a new height for Everest of 8,848.86 metres in 2020.

Why is K2 called the Savage Mountain?

It has steep, exposed climbing, fierce weather and a high fatality rate, so even though it is lower than Everest, many climbers consider it harder.

Which eight-thousander is the most dangerous?

Annapurna I has historically had one of the highest fatality rates, with K2 and Nanga Parbat also known for difficulty. Statistics change as more people climb.

Are records officially verified?

There is no single governing body. Records are usually documented by expedition reports, the Himalayan Database and mountaineering media, and some claims are disputed.
