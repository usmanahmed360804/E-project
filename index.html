DISCOVER THE ADVENTURE

What is Mountaineering?

Mountaineering, also known as mountain climbing, is the sport or activity of climbing mountains. It combines physical strength, technical skills, planning and knowledge of the mountain environment.

Modern mountaineering includes climbing rock, snow and ice-covered mountains. Climbers must understand weather, navigation, shelter, equipment and emergency procedures.

A BRIEF HISTORY

How Mountaineering Began

1786

Mont Blanc

Jacques Balmat and Michel-Gabriel Paccard reach the summit of Mont Blanc, the highest peak in the Alps. Many historians mark this as the start of modern mountaineering.

1865

The Matterhorn

Edward Whymper's team makes the first ascent of the Matterhorn. Four climbers die on the descent, shaking public views of the sport.

1950

Annapurna I

A French expedition led by Maurice Herzog becomes the first to climb an 8,000-metre peak.

1953

Everest and Nanga Parbat

Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay reach the top of Everest. Later the same year, Hermann Buhl makes a solo summit of Nanga Parbat.

1954

K2

An Italian expedition makes the first ascent of K2, the second highest mountain in the world.

1978

Without Oxygen

Reinhold Messner and Peter Habeler climb Everest without supplemental oxygen, proving it was possible.

CHOOSE YOUR PATH

Types of Mountaineering

Hiking & Trekking

Walking on trails to high viewpoints and passes. It needs little technical gear and is the usual first step into the mountains.

Beginner

Rock Climbing

Climbing cliffs and crags using hands, feet, ropes and protection. Skills here carry over directly to alpine routes.

Intermediate

Ice & Snow Climbing

Using crampons and ice axes on glaciers, frozen waterfalls and steep snow slopes.

Intermediate

Alpine Climbing

Combines rock, snow and ice on one route, usually in a light and fast style over one or more days.

Advanced

Expedition Climbing

Multi-week climbs on high peaks with base camps, fixed ropes and careful acclimatization.

Advanced

Ski Mountaineering

Climbing on skis with skins, then skiing down. It needs strong avalanche knowledge.

SKILLS & GEAR

Essential Techniques

  • Rope work and belaying

    Tying in, managing the rope and catching a fall are the base of every roped climb.

  • Ice axe self-arrest

    Stopping a slide on a snow slope by rolling onto the axe and digging in the pick.

  • Crampon technique

    Placing all points flat on the ice or snow, and walking with wide feet to avoid snagging.

  • Glacier travel and crevasse rescue

    Moving roped together, probing for hidden crevasses and hauling a fallen partner out.

  • Acclimatization

    Climb high, sleep low. Ascend gradually so the body can adapt to thinner air.

  • Navigation

    Reading maps, using a compass and GPS, and planning a route with turnaround times.

Basic Gear Checklist

  • Helmet and climbing harness
  • Mountaineering boots and crampons
  • Ice axe and trekking poles
  • Rope, carabiners and slings
  • Layered clothing, shell jacket and insulated gloves
  • Goggles, sunglasses and sunscreen
  • Headlamp with spare batteries
  • Map, compass and GPS or phone with offline maps
  • Stove, food, water and emergency shelter
  • First aid kit and a satellite communicator

RESPECT THE MOUNTAIN

Hazards & Safety

Avalanches

Check the avalanche bulletin, avoid steep loaded slopes after snowfall, and carry a beacon, probe and shovel.

Altitude Sickness

Headache, nausea and poor sleep can signal acute mountain sickness. Severe forms, HAPE and HACE, are life threatening and require descent.

Cold Injuries

Hypothermia and frostbite develop quickly in wind and cold. Keep dry, eat often and cover exposed skin.

Sudden Weather

Storms, whiteouts and strong wind can arrive within hours. Start early and set a firm turnaround time.

Crevasses & Rockfall

Hidden crevasses and falling rocks cause many accidents. Rope up on glaciers and wear a helmet.

Human Error

Summit fever, fatigue and poor planning are common factors. Be willing to turn back.

BEFORE YOU START

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I start mountaineering?

Begin with hiking and scrambling to build fitness, then take a basic mountaineering course covering ropes, ice axe and crampon skills with a certified guide.

How fit do I need to be?

Good aerobic endurance and leg strength matter most. Training with long hikes carrying a loaded pack is the best preparation.

Do I need supplemental oxygen?

Most climbers use oxygen above about 8,000 metres. Below that, proper acclimatization is usually enough.

When is the best season to climb?

It depends on the range. Himalayan peaks are commonly climbed in spring and autumn, the Karakoram in summer, and the Alps in summer for most routes.

Should I climb with a guide?

Beginners should. A guide manages route finding, weather decisions and rescue, and teaches skills you will use later on your own.
